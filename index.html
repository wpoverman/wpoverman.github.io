Bio

My research develops methods with formal guarantees for AI safety and alignment, especially scalable oversight: how humans can monitor, correct, and retain meaningful control over increasingly capable AI systems. Recent work studies how weaker overseers can constrain stronger agents [ICML’26], how human–agent protocols can balance safety and autonomy [ICML’26], risk-controlled model alignment [NeurIPS’24, NeurIPS’25], and causal evaluation in interacting human–AI populations [ICML’26 Workshop]. I draw on tools from reinforcement learning, uncertainty quantification, causal inference, and game theory.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Operations, Information, and Technology at the Stanford Graduate School of Business, advised by Mohsen Bayati. I successfully defended my dissertation in May 2026. Before Stanford, I was a visiting researcher at the Institute for Basic Science in South Korea, earned an M.S. in Computer Science from UC Irvine, and completed a B.S. in Mathematics and Computer Science at Caltech.

I also work on reinforcement learning and sequential decision-making [RLC’26, ICLR’22], network experimentation [NeurIPS’24], and healthcare operations. I have also interned at Uber, applying reinforcement learning and causal inference to problems in the ridesharing and delivery marketplaces.
